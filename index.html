Not Kansas 2

The inside of the moon is hyperbolic space: its wall is infinitely far away. In Not Kansas there was one inside. Here every level has its own.

Each level's moon holds a different honeycomb: cubes five round an edge, then dodecahedra, icosahedra, dodecahedra again. Those four are all the regular honeycombs hyperbolic space has with finite cells. After them come four whose corners lie on the wall at infinity. Then the round begins again, in a new outside world.

At the centre of every cell there is a pearl. Fly into one and you come out in the next level's outside world, the one whose crystal you saw in the cells.

Two crystals grow at two scales: a coarse one builds the bodies, and a fine lattice grows inside them, so every body is made of smaller crystal that feeds back on it.

The moon is the light. Crystal casts shadows, and lit crystal grows, so the crystals compete for the moon.

The moon is also a membrane. The world outside and the world inside are glued across it, so crystal that touches it grows through, and inside the inversion bends it into arcs falling toward the core.

Where you fly you leave a wake that crystal grows in. Give it your camera and your picture becomes the sky and the light inside the glass.
